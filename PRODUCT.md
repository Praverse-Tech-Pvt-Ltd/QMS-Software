# Product

## Register

product

## Users

QA executives, QC analysts, QA managers and heads of quality at a pharmaceutical manufacturing site. They work at desks on office monitors, often for a full shift, moving through queues of deviations, CAPAs, change controls, documents, training records and approvals. Many records carry regulatory weight (21 CFR Part 11, EU GMP Annex 11), so they read carefully and sign electronically.

## Product Purpose

NexGen QMS is the system of record for a site's quality events and controlled documents. It replaces paper and spreadsheet workflows with traceable records, audit trails and e-signatures. Success means a QA user can find what needs their attention, act on it correctly, and prove it later to an inspector, without the tool getting in the way.

## Brand Personality

Precise, dependable, quiet. The interface should feel like a well-kept instrument: dense with information, never noisy. Confidence comes from clarity and consistency, not decoration.

## Anti-references

- Stock SaaS templates: purple-to-blue gradients, frosted glass cards, floating blobs, hover lift on every element.
- "Hero metric" dashboards with oversized numbers and decorative trend chips.
- Click effects, confetti, or any motion that does not report a state change.
- Legacy enterprise QMS screens (cramped grey forms with no hierarchy). Dense must still be readable.

## Design Principles

1. **Information first.** Every screen exists to show records and what to do with them; layout serves scanning and comparison.
2. **Earned familiarity.** Standard patterns (side nav, tables, forms, tabs) behave exactly as expected. No invented affordances.
3. **State is always explicit.** Status, severity, due dates and who acts next are visible without hovering or opening a record.
4. **Calm under load.** High density with clear hierarchy: one accent for actions, semantic color only for status.
5. **Audit-ready.** Nothing ambiguous: labels are exact, actions are named for what they do, and destructive or signing actions are unmistakable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text at least 4.5:1 contrast, visible keyboard focus on every control, labelled form fields, status never conveyed by color alone, and `prefers-reduced-motion` respected.
